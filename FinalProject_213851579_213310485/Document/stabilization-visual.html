Context

Where stabilization sits in the project

It is the first block of the pipeline. Every later stage inherits its output — so its job is to make the background as static as possible.

The four project blocks. Stabilization runs first and feeds everything downstream.
🎯

Why quality matters here: background subtraction models the background over time. If the camera still shakes — or slowly drifts — background pixels move and get misclassified as foreground. A rock-solid, drift-free stabilization makes every later stage easier.

Core idea

Align every frame directly to the first frame

Instead of smoothing the camera path (classic stabilization), we cancel camera motion entirely: each frame is warped into the coordinate system of frame 0. The background freezes; only the person moves.

Warp-to-first-frame: cancel camera motion instead of smoothing it.
🧊

Frozen background

Ideal input for temporal-median background modeling in the next stage.

🖤

Black borders — fine

Warping reveals pixels outside the frame. Spec FAQ #1 explicitly allows them.

🎨

Color preserved

Motion is estimated on grayscale internally, but the warp is applied to the full BGR frame. Exactly one bilinear warp per frame — no quality lost for matting.

Background

What is a homography?

A homography (projective transform) is the most general mapping between two images of the same plane — or between any two images taken by a camera that only rotated. It is a 3×3 matrix acting on homogeneous coordinates.

[ x′, y′, w ]T = H · [ x, y, 1 ]T    ⇒    xnew = x′/w ,   ynew = y′/w The perspective divide by w is what similarity/affine transforms don't have — it lets far parts of the image shrink and near parts grow (keystone). H has 9 entries but scale doesn't matter (H and 3H map identically), so 8 degrees of freedom → 4 point correspondences determine it.
Transform hierarchy. Each step adds freedom; only the homography can make one side of the image shrink relative to the other.
📐

When is it exact?

Two cases: (1) the scene is a plane (wall, floor); (2) the camera only rotates around its center — no translation. Handheld shake is almost pure rotation with tiny translation, and our background is wall-dominated → a homography describes the frame-to-frame background motion almost perfectly.

🤳

Why we need the 8th DOF here

Tilting the camera up/down or left/right (out-of-plane rotation) makes one edge of the image shrink — keystone. Similarity/affine cannot represent that, so a residual misalignment remains at the image edges. We measured this: see the benchmark section.

The algorithm

Anchored KLT tracking — what happens every frame

A pool of corners is tracked continuously through the video. Each corner remembers its anchor: the frame-0 position it corresponds to. The fit is always current frame → frame 0, directly.

1

Track the pool  ·  cv2.calcOpticalFlowPyrLK

Pyramidal Lucas-Kanade moves every corner from the previous frame into the current one (same algorithm we implemented in ex2 — coarse-to-fine pyramid, iterative refinement — the OpenCV version is used for runtime). Corners that fail to track are dropped, and their anchors with them.

2

Fit homography to the anchors  ·  cv2.findHomography + RANSAC

One robust fit maps current corner positions onto their frame-0 anchors. This is the whole trick: the transform to frame 0 is estimated directly, not assembled from 204 consecutive steps — so per-frame estimation errors have nothing to accumulate into. Drift is impossible by construction.

3

Prune outliers  ·  RANSAC inlier mask

Points that disagree with the consensus motion — corners on the walking person, badly tracked corners — are removed from the pool. The person cannot poison future fits: his corners survive at most a frame or two.

4

Warp the color frame  ·  cv2.warpPerspective

The original BGR frame is warped once, bilinearly, into frame-0 coordinates. Out-of-frame regions become black (allowed). If any step above failed, the previous frame's transform is reused — graceful degradation, no crash on the secret test video.

5

Replenish the pool  ·  cv2.goodFeaturesToTrack with a mask

When fewer than 200 corners survive, fresh Shi-Tomasi corners are detected away from existing ones. A new corner's anchor is its position mapped to frame-0 coordinates through the current fit — it immediately joins the voting pool.

The key design choice

Direct fit vs composed chain

The naive approach estimates frame-to-frame motion and multiplies 204 matrices. Every estimate carries ~0.1 px of noise — integrated over the video it becomes visible drift that slowly smears the background.

Corners are tracked frame-to-frame, but the FIT is always against frame-0 anchors — tracking chains carry the correspondence, not the transform.
⚓

Why this beats a "drift-correction" pass: an alternative we tested estimates frame-to-frame motion, composes it, then corrects the drift by re-registering each warped frame to frame 0 — two LK passes and two warps per frame. The anchored fit achieves better alignment with half the work, because it never creates drift in the first place.

Deep dive 1

Lucas-Kanade in one minute

LK assumes a pixel keeps its brightness as it moves, and that all pixels in a small window move together.

Ix·u + Iy·v + It = 0 Brightness constancy, linearized. One equation, two unknowns (u, v) — underdetermined for a single pixel.
🪟

Window assumption

Take a 21×21 window around the point: 441 equations, still 2 unknowns. Solve by least squares. This is exactly the ex2 lucas_kanade_step.

🔺

Pyramid for big motion

Linearization only holds for ~1px motion. Downscale the image repeatedly, solve at the coarsest level, propagate and refine level by level. Handles the large jitters of handheld video.

📍

Corners only

Flow is fully determined only where the gradient is strong in two directions (no aperture problem) — Shi-Tomasi corners. ~600 points instead of 2M pixels: ~1000× less work than the dense ex2 flow, and all the global fit needs.

📚

For the report: pyramidal LK is course material (ex2) — mention it, no explanation required. findHomography + RANSAC and the anchored-tracking design must be explained (spec page 6: explain anything not studied or modified) — the homography and RANSAC sections of this page are that explanation.

Deep dive 2

RANSAC: ignoring the walking person

The person moves differently from the background. Averaging all motion vectors lets him drag the estimate. RANSAC votes him out — and our pool pruning then evicts his corners entirely.

One frame's motion field. RANSAC finds the homography most corners agree on; the person's few vectors can't win the vote.
🎲

1 · Sample

Pick a minimal random subset of correspondences (4 pairs fix a homography) and fit a candidate.

🗳️

2 · Vote

Count how many other correspondences agree with the candidate within 3 px (inliers).

🏆

3 · Refit

Repeat many times, keep the candidate with the most inliers, then least-squares refit on all inliers only.

How we chose

Measured, not guessed

Every design variant was benchmarked on INPUT.avi with three metrics: mean absolute difference between consecutive frames (central crop — lower = steadier), variance of Laplacian of the temporal mean frame (higher = background more static), and runtime.

The decisive experiment: swapping only the model in the anchored tracker (similarity → homography) moved sharpness 26.5 → 71.3. That isolates the cause: handheld shake contains out-of-plane rotation, whose keystone residual needs the homography's 8th degree of freedom. Every other knob (corner count, block size, corner source) moved the numbers by almost nothing.

🛡️

Fallbacks everywhere: tracking failure, too few corners, or RANSAC returning nothing → the previous transform is reused and the pool is re-seeded. The frame passes through; no crash on the secret test video.

💧

Streaming, single warp: frames are read, warped once (bilinear), and written one at a time — no full-video buffer in RAM, no repeated resampling → no quality loss for matting.

⚠️

Model assumption to state in the report: a single homography is exact only for a planar scene or a purely rotating camera. Our background has multiple depths, so tiny parallax exists; RANSAC locks onto the dominant background plane and treats parallax as outlier noise. The benchmark shows this approximation costs nothing measurable on this footage.
